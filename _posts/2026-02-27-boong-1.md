# Abstract
Lately, I've been getting really interested in *build system* — and I don't mean the usual **Makefile vs. CMake** conversations
\
\
What caught my attention is the **distinctive** side of the build world: tools like **Scons** and **Bazel**, which feel like they come with their own philosophy about how builds *should* work
\
And then there are the systems that were built for very specific ecosystems — **GN** and **Soong**, both developed at Google at famously used in large-scale projects: GN for **Chromium** or **Fuchsia**, and Soong for **AOSP**
\
![I love google](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQeY2Q1Q7nvME6PNOKMFo16bplbktFP-ATsuQ&s)
\
\
I've been especially drawn to **GN** and **Soong** — and if I had to pick one, it'd definitely be **Soong**
\
\
Because of the kind of work I do, I end up poking through the **entire AOSP source tree** pretty often
\
And somewhere along the way, I started to develop this weird fondness for Soong's world — especially **Blueprint**
\
There's something about Blueprint's syntax that feels *almost deceptively simple*: minimal, straightforward, and surprisingly intuitive once you get used to it
\
It deosn't try too hard to be fancy, any maybe that's exacetly why it's so appealing
\
![I love Android](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTaXG_evH1NmCOkHfxJMgSSoTSyFOu31SpLZA&s)
\
\
So this post is going to be the starting point of a small side project: I'm going to build a tiny build tool called **Boong**, inspired by Soong and powered by Google's Blueprint format
\
It won't be a **"Next Bazel"** or anything ambitious like that — more like a lightweight experiment to explore what makes Soong feel so clean, and what it ould look like to recreate that vibe in a much smaller, friendlier package
\
\
This of it as a way to learn by building: one step at a time, guided by Blueprint's simplicity and Soong's design choices.
